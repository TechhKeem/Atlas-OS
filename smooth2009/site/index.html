Who we are

Educating, developing, organizing, and empowering since 2009

S.M.O.O.T.H. Inc. is a membership-based social development organization. We provide social and professional development, mentoring, life skills training, community service, and entrepreneurial skill development for young men and marginalized youth of color.

Four friends founded the organization at Morgan State University on October 28, 2009. They saw too few all-male organizations that underclassmen could join, and too few male student leaders on campus. Their answer was a space where any young man could create, develop, and carry out his ideas, built on peer mentoring, accountability partners, and group learning.

The members soon took that work into Baltimore, mentoring and tutoring middle and high school boys facing hard environments. Today S.M.O.O.T.H. connects college men, university staff, and the wider community across Baltimore and Philadelphia.

Mission

To educate, develop, organize, and empower young males and marginalized youth of color to improve their graduation and retention rates, and to build a transnational network of young men who use ethics, collective action, and social entrepreneurship to create lasting social change.

Vision

A community with a clear pipeline from primary school to college for young men of color, despite poverty, educational obstacles, or juvenile justice involvement, where they go on to create and lead their own businesses.

Objectives

  • Foster the development of young men of color in schools and colleges
  • Partner across public, private, nonprofit, and international sectors
  • Build character through quality mentorship
  • Offer educational, cultural, civic, and social programs
  • Build capacity to lead small and medium enterprises

What we do

National programs

Every section runs the same core programs, so a member at Temple and a member at Coppin share the same standards and the same work.

Members

Service-Based Leadership Development

College members serve one-year terms on the executive board, on committees, and in the general body, planning and running campus events, programs, and service projects.

Youth

Jr. SMOOTH

Each college section adopts a K–12 school in Baltimore or Philadelphia and mentors its young men throughout the year.

Symposium

R.U.L.E.S. for Black Men

Restoring Uplift Leadership Education and Success: an intergenerational, men-only symposium to inspire, educate, connect, and unite Black and brown men.

Every October

An annual week of social events and community service marking the organization's founding on October 28.

The founders

Four Morgan men, one idea

Dwight D. Flowers

Co-founder · Brooklyn, NY

Known as DJ Flow, Brooklyn-born Dwight Flowers learned the value of hard work early. Raised by a single mother, he grew up committed to serving his community and providing for others. At Morgan State University in 2009 he started two things: S.M.O.O.T.H. and his career as a DJ. Within six years he had become one of the most sought-after DJs in the DMV.

Fab Empire named him Baltimore DJ of the Year in 2013, and the Baltimore Sun gave him the same title in 2019. He has performed at colleges and stadiums across the country and for clients including Nicki Minaj, Michelle Obama, Khloé Kardashian, and Jay-Z. A member of the Core DJs coalition, he has been a voice of DTLR Radio and an official DJ for Shy Glizzy, Roc Nation's Casanova, and Tate Kobang.

DaQuan M. Lawrence, MIPP

Co-founder · Harlem, NY

DaQuan Lawrence is an international humanitarian, human rights advocate, and civil servant from Harlem, New York. At the Urban Institute's Health Policy Center he has supported research on substance use and opioid use disorders, the Affordable Care Act, Medicaid and Medicare, and social and economic inequality, and he developed a cultural competency toolkit for research on populations of African descent for Urban's Diversity and Inclusion Steering Committee.

His graduate studies focused on international public policy, human rights, humanitarian research, and economics. His work has taken him to Washington, DC for policy and advocacy; to Baltimore for education policy, community outreach, and racial equity; to Indonesia to study labor practices; to Haiti and the Dominican Republic to research migration, displacement, and statelessness; and to Liberia, where he worked with vulnerable women and ex-combatant youth during the Ebola response. Born into foster care in New York City to an orphaned teenage mother, he centers marginalized people in all of his work.

Hakeem T. Hooker

Co-founder · Philadelphia, PA

Born in Philadelphia and raised in Delaware, Hakeem Hooker arrived at Morgan State University in the spring of 2008 with a dual culinary diploma from high school. He majored in hospitality management with a minor in marketing, and an internship with the campus food service company became a part-time job that lasted until graduation. At 25 he became a food service director, managing multi-million-dollar accounts and serving more than 1,600 people per meal period.

In 2016, at 28, he founded DinnerClub Hospitality LLC, a corporate and celebrity catering company. Its clients have included Downtown Locker Room, Morgan State University, the Be Organization, and Banner Hill, as well as artists, athletes, and public figures such as 2 Chainz, Meek Mill, Roc Nation A&R Rel Carter, journalist Kevin Blackistone, and author Dawn West. He continues to build DinnerClub while devoting his time and effort to the growth of S.M.O.O.T.H. Inc.

Frederick D. Watkins Jr.

Co-founder · Baltimore, MD

Fred Watkins Jr. is a comedian, event producer, and host from Baltimore, Maryland. At Morgan State University he found a passion for community service and mentorship that led him to found two companies of his own.

He founded Lil' Laughs, an anti-bullying campaign that reshapes the idea of the "class clown" and counters bullying through comedy and mentorship. Through BIG Laughs Company LLC he works with comedians across the country, and he has appeared on Oxygen's "Last Squad Standing."
